# Medical Content Policy

Nyla provides menstrual-health education, not diagnosis or individualized medical treatment.

## Source hierarchy

Claims should be grounded primarily in:

1. public-health authorities and national health services
2. professional medical societies and formal clinical guidance
3. systematic reviews / consensus statements
4. high-quality peer-reviewed evidence when authoritative guidance does not cover the topic

Lifestyle folklore, influencer content and unsourced wellness claims are not acceptable evidence.

## Card requirements

Every educational card must have:

- a stable ID
- concise flashcard copy that can stand alone
- expanded explanation
- practical steps only when supported by evidence/guidance
- clear distinction between common experiences and warning signs
- at least one source URL
- source organization / publication
- source access date
- `medical_review_status`
- `content_version`
- `last_reviewed`

## Language

Use calm, ordinary language. Avoid frightening wording, infantilization, shame, euphemisms that obscure anatomy, and claims that a symptom is "normal" when the evidence only supports that it is common.

Prefer:

- "This can happen during a period."
- "If pain is stopping you from doing your usual activities, medical assessment is worth seeking."

Avoid:

- "Don't worry, this is totally normal."
- diagnoses based on logs
- deterministic hormonal explanations for mood or behavior

## Personalization boundary

The app may surface a relevant card after a user logs a symptom. That is content selection, not a diagnosis.

Pattern insights should say what was observed:

> "You logged headaches in the three days before 5 of your last 6 periods."

They should not convert the correlation into a causal/diagnostic statement.

## Excluded content

The knowledge library does not contain pregnancy, conception, masturbation, sexual coaching or pleasure modules.

An article may mention that a missed period has multiple possible causes and advise seeking appropriate medical assessment. It must not become a pregnancy-tracking flow.
